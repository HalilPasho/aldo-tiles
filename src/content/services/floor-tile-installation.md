## A floor is only as good as what sits under it

Walk across a room and feel for bounce. If a wood subfloor flexes under your weight, tile and grout will eventually crack, however neatly they are laid. So our first visit is less about tile and more about the structure: joists, plywood thickness, high spots and old adhesive. If the floor needs stiffening or levelling, you will hear about it before work starts, not as a surprise halfway through.

Concrete has its own habits. Basement slabs often dip toward a floor drain and carry hairline cracks from when they cured. We level the low areas, then use an uncoupling membrane where it is needed, so that movement in the slab stays out of the finished surface.

## Large tile takes more care

Big porcelain slabs and long wood-look planks are popular for good reason, since they make a room feel calm and open. They also show every flaw. A small hump that a 12-inch tile would hide will leave a long plank with a raised edge you can catch with a sock. This uneven edge is called lippage, and we control it with a flatter base, full mortar coverage and levelling clips.

Layout matters as well. Long tile usually looks and performs best with a one-third offset, because a half offset lines up the slight bow found in most long tiles. We will show you how that looks on your floor before anything is set.

## Patterns and transitions

We dry-lay every floor from the centre of the room, so doorways and focal points get full tile and the small cuts stay along the edges. For a herringbone, we set the pattern on the line you see first when you walk in. We also plan the details that make a floor feel finished: heights at hardwood and carpet joins, door clearances, floor vents and soft movement joints at the walls.

## Rooms we tile across Mississauga

Front entries take snow, salt and boots through a long Ontario winter, so we recommend a dense porcelain with some texture. Kitchens and laundry rooms need a grout that handles spills. Basements benefit from the warmth of a heated mat, which also takes the chill off bare feet in January.
