## The part of a shower you never see

Tile and grout do not keep water out. Water passes through grout lines and reaches whatever sits behind them, which is how a shower can look fine for years while the framing behind it quietly rots. A shower that lasts starts with the layers nobody will ever look at again.

Before any tile goes up, we check that the walls are plumb, the framing is solid and the drain sits where it should. Then we build the wet area as one sealed system: a base sloped toward the drain, a membrane carried up the walls, and sealed corners, niches and pipe openings. Where the design allows it, we flood test the base for a day before tiling, so that a leak shows up while it is still cheap to fix.

## A layout planned around your room

A bathroom is small enough that every cut is on show. We draw the layout first, centring tile on the niche and the main wall, carrying grout lines from wall to floor where we can, and placing narrow cuts where the eye won't land. Niches and benches are framed to suit the tile, rather than the other way around.

Your plumber sets the valve and drain, and we agree those positions before the walls close, so the shower trim lands cleanly on a full tile instead of a patched corner.

## Tile that suits a wet room

Porcelain is the easiest choice for most showers because it absorbs almost no water. Marble and other natural stone look wonderful but need sealing and gentle cleaners, and we will tell you plainly if a stone is a poor match for how your family uses the room. For shower floors, smaller tile gives more grip and follows the slope to the drain.

## Working in Mississauga homes and condos

Many older Mississauga bathrooms still have their original tub surrounds, often tiled over drywall with no real waterproofing. Replacing one is a good chance to turn a dated tub into a walk-in shower. In condos we also work within elevator bookings, permitted work hours and the building's renovation rules, so let us know your property manager's requirements early.
